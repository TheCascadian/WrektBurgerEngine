# Changelog

## 2026-10-19
- Less lag in play: no tick is a hitch any more. The daily work is spread over the hours (visibility and events at hour 0, a 24th of the armies and of the AI's countries each hour, production, navy, air, economy, diplomacy and politics at hours 2 and 3) and the monthly work over the first nine days, each system on a day of its own and country by country in 24 hourly parts (population, economy, politics) or 672 hourly slots (the AI's monthly turn). On a 1530 start the worst tick fell from about 7 s to 0.14 s and the 1 % low from 8 to 23 frames per second; a 1938 start runs at 60. Projects whose AI overrides `DailyTick` or `MonthlyTick` still get them once, at hour 0 of day 1.
- Faster simulation: the modifier of politics no longer scans all states for each country each hour (core land held by others is counted once per hour), the AI's border lists are built from a fixed list of land borders, reachability of a division's target is found by flooding only the region asked about, a peace-time army finds its neighbours from that list, the AI colonist looks at the nearest free states only, the institutions' catch-up runs in the background, and the units at war no longer ask the modifier system per division. About 40 % less simulation time per day on a 1530 start.
- Less garbage: the country names no longer allocate a string per letter and four copies of it per frame (400 KB a frame down to 140), and the layout input reuses the neighbour lists. `Program.cs` asks for the sustained-low-latency collector; `burger run` and the Studio start the game with `DOTNET_GCgen0size=8000000` (a 128 MB youngest generation: a tenth of the collections, a third of the pause time). Start the game from another place with that variable set to get the same.
- The Play page of the main menu (the start dates) no longer sorts all the countries and makes the flag of every one of them each frame: the list is sorted once per map and only the rows in view are drawn.
- Starting a game is about four times faster in the worlds with hundreds of countries: the institutions' months of spreading before the start date are counted in the background (whatever reads the presence waits for them), the fusing of states removes them in one pass, the AI colonist only looks at free states within its range, a country's lost core land is counted once per day for all, and the AI looks at its neighbours, enemies and a sample of the others (above 200 countries) instead of every country.
- Measuring: `burger validate <project> --start 1530 --settle 2500 --tick-stats --profile` prints the slowest ticks with their date and hour, the garbage per day by scope, the time of each modifier provider and the slowest country calls; `--preview 1000,1530` times the start screen's date switch; `Burger.Game --perf` also prints the allocations by stage of the frame. `burger validate <project> --start 1530` loads a start date as the menu does; `--preview 1000,1530` times the start screen's date switch, `--settle ms` waits before simulating, `--profile` also lists the time of each scope.
- Fixed: the Habsburg Netherlands no longer merge with Austria (one tag with the Low Countries named "Habsburg Netherlands" showed over Hungary at 1530); Royal Hungary and Transylvania added from 1530 to 1700.

## 2026-10-18
- Province-exact borders: the map is rebuilt from Natural Earth admin-1 units (38,536 locations, `tools/geo`), and the gazetteer gains polygon (`P`) and province (`U`) realms, with hand-made realms for Iberia, France, Italy, Britain and Ireland, Germany and the Low Countries, northeast Europe and the Balkans, the Near East and Maghreb, China, Korea, Japan, India and Central Asia (`units_*.txt`).
- Impassable states: 469 states in the Sahara, Libyan Desert, Rub' al Khali, Taklamakan, Lut/Kavir, Atacama, Simpson Desert, Changtang and the Greenland ice sheet (and states walled in by them) are `type = impassable`; units cannot enter, countries may own them but not consist of them alone.
- States per start date: the map keeps 25,000 fine states, and each snapshot lists which touching states of one owner fuse when a game starts on it (about 8,600 states per date, groups of about 6 locations). Saves record the fused groups; the start screen still previews the fine map; the map editor refuses to write a fused world.
- Start dates audited date by date: duplicate and anachronistic names fixed (about 150 renames in `tools/gazetteer/renames.txt`), the dataset's umbrella rule no longer splits Austria-Hungary, the UAE and the Papal States, colonial Africa at 1900, 1 January 1938 and 1945 for Austria and Czechoslovakia, the Caribbean, Madagascar and New Guinea before their colonies. `tools/audit_strays.py` and `tools/audit_major.py` find what is left; stray pieces of peoples and small holes join the state around them. The history build is 3 times faster (numpy gazetteer, cached lookups) and `tools/rebuild_history.sh` rebuilds everything with step times.
- `tools/map_owners.py <year> <png>` prints a full coloured map of who owns what at a date (crop, scale, labels) to compare with online atlases.
- Start dates before 1500 rebuilt from a gazetteer of real peoples and realms (`tools/gazetteer`): about 2,000 peoples, kingdoms and cities with their years, seeds and radii, written from Wikipedia articles, Barrington, Putzger, Tan Qixiang, Schwartzberg, UNESCO General History of Africa, Handbook of North American Indians and other atlases (listed at the top of each file). Peoples sit where they lived (no more Khoikhoi in the Congo or Choctaw in Europe), the small states inside empires exist (Anatolian beyliks, Rus principalities, Irish and Welsh kingdoms, Maya cities, Swahili city-states, Nubian kingdoms...), and polities of one or two states are kept.
- States are cut along the borders of every date: `tools/refine_states.py ... model` plans the cuts from the whole model, `burger split-states --plan` applies them, `burger export-location-centres` feeds it. World500BCE has 10,905 states.
- Tools: `history_probe.py` (who held a place at each date), `audit_history.py`, `audit_clusters.py`, `audit_empty.py`, `audit_dupnames.py`, `plot_history.py`.

## 2026-10-16
- New countries get names that fit what they are. Each ideology lists `country_names` formats in `common/ideologies` ("Kingdom of {region}", "{culture} Warbands", "{region} Commons"; `{region}` is the land, `{culture}` the people); the first one no living country has is used, long area names are skipped, and a numeral is added only as a last resort. Breakaway and rebel countries both use them (no more "Commoner Proto-Germanic Republic" or "... of Siberia South of the Taiga 4"). Ideologies carry a `government` word that a country takes at the start if it had none, and when the ideology takes power.
- Overextension: a realm above `overextension_states` (15) states loses its peoples faster (independence movements gain `overextension_independence` more speed per extra block of that size, up to `overextension_cap` times). World500BCE also raises `independence_speed` to 0.9. In a 50-year run this gives about 50 breakaways and 200 wars instead of 6 breakaways, with the world ending at 80 countries instead of 45.
- `burger validate` also prints the new countries, the ruling ideologies and the most held reforms.
- Fixed: divisions never marched to their front posts in BCE games. The retry timer compared the (negative) BCE day count with a default of 0, so every order was skipped as "try again later". Armies now move to the front at war.
- AI armies at peace garrison the border with a neighbour that is not an ally or family (one neighbour per army) instead of sitting in the capital. `burger validate` prints how many divisions move, idle, and why.
- An observer (no player country) now sees "X is now a colony of Y" in the notifications.
- Characters now take names from their country's culture. Order: names set on the country, then `common/name_lists` for the country's `culture`, then the default list. `NamesFor` in `CharacterSystem` covers loaded, random and born characters. A name list block may carry `cultures = { a b c }` to serve several cultures from one pool (the block key still serves itself). World500BCE has a list for every culture id (126 keys; at least 50 male, 50 female and 50 family names each; multi-word names in double quotes). OPEN: 500 BCE records exist for few of these peoples. Where none exist, the names come from the closest recorded or descendant language, not from attested 500 BCE sources.
- Four new features (packages in `assets/features`, added to World500BCE): `culture_traditions` (three traditions per culture, cultures that share a state for 50 years become a new culture), `government_reforms` (up to 5 reforms per government in `common/governments`, 7 national ideas per set in `common/ideas`, corruption 0-100 with events at 50), `succession` (primogeniture, ultimogeniture, elective, gavelkind in the Laws menu; regents for rulers under 16) and `dynasties` (prestige, bastards, concubines under the concubinage law, injury, plague and fever). Core in `Burger.Core/Society`, numbers in `Game.Rules.Society`, new command `reform` for multiplayer, new Realm panel (key `K`), `burger society-test <project>`. See docs/game/MODDING.md.
- Reforms, national ideas and corruption moved into the Laws tab. Laws unlock by age like ideologies (`unlock` on a law group or law; `unlock_law`, `law_unlocked`); World500BCE offers only laws of its age at the start (press 1450, standing army 221 BCE, mercantile policy 1500, national conscription and industrial policy 1800).
- Character sheet: clicking a character anywhere (roster, heirs, family) opens a window with identity, where they live (with a show-on-map button), abilities, traits, illness and the whole family; relatives open in turn with a back button.
- The Realm panel is gone: culture traditions are in the People panel, reforms/ideas/corruption and succession/dynasty are tabs of the Politics panel. Countries without elections or an assembly get no Parties tab and an Overview without a parliament.
- Ideologies can be locked: `unlock = { year / technology }` in `common/ideologies`, effect `unlock_ideology`, trigger `ideology_unlocked`, `assembly = yes`. World500BCE has the ideologies of its age (Civic, Royalist, Warrior, Leveler, Aristocratic, Priestly) and eight that appear later (Legalist, Imperial, Feudal, Mercantile, Absolutist, Liberal, Nationalist, Socialist).
- Every ruler gets a spouse, children, parents and siblings at the start of a game (`GenerateFamilies`). Spending political power on reforms, laws, ministers and crackdowns asks for confirmation first.
- Fixed: the `development` save section wrote its presence list with doubled quotes and failed to load.
- A block in `common/governments` that has only `reforms` or `start_reforms` no longer replaces the starting politics of that government.

## 2026-10-15
- Third security pass: mod code reachable from network data is metered. The mod compiler inserts a step meter (`Fuel.Tick`) into every function, loop, catch, finally and label of sandboxed scripts (`FuelRewriter`); `validate` and `apply` run under a step and memory budget on the calling thread, and the stop cannot be caught. Steps are counted, so an `apply` stops at the same point on every machine. The abandoned-thread watchdog is gone. `sec-test` runs eight `validate` attacks that ignore the stop and an `apply` that never returns, on host and client, and checks no thread or busy core is left. Iterators (`yield return`) now pass the sandbox. A host save above a warn level loads with a warning. `burger save-limits <project> --years N` measures saves after N years of AI play: `World500BCE_Maxi_100` reaches 31 MB after 10 years; `World500BCE_Maxi_800` reaches 98 MB after 1 year and is refused (OPEN: parser and save size work next). Channel marked UNREVIEWED; Game window explicitly out of scope.
- Second security pass (branch `multiplayer-security`): the channel is now TLS 1.3 plus a key confirmation bound to the host certificate (spec in docs/multiplayer/channel-spec.md; the own ECDH/AES-GCM channel is gone). Host listener limits per address and a ban after repeated failed handshakes. Per-player tokens with expiry, single live use, revocation, and session secret rotation. Saves sent by a host are parsed strictly (`SaveGuard`, `ScriptParser.ParseStrict` with depth, token, node and string limits; the old parser had unbounded recursion) and the client leaves on an invalid one. A false checksum gets a full save at most once per 30 s per peer, then strikes. Mod `validate` has a 100 ms deadline and a circuit breaker. The allowed mod API was audited and pruned (docs/game/modding-api.md). `MpController` (Burger.Core) holds the multiplayer glue of the Game app, so it can be tested without a window. `sec-test` grew from 18 to about 70 sandbox escapes plus channel attacks (replay, reflection, downgrade, MITM, truncation, flood), hostile saves, false checksum flood, tokens, validate deadline and the app path. New docs: channel-spec, os-sandbox-evaluation, external-review-plan.
- Multiplayer security and modding (branch `multiplayer-security`), see docs/multiplayer/multiplayer-security.md, docs/game/modding-api.md and SECURITY_MODDING_TRADEOFFS.md. Protocol 2. Every TCP link is encrypted and authenticated (ECDH P-256 plus a session secret from the join code, HKDF, AES-GCM with strict counters); join codes are now 25 characters. Every received line is checked (`Wire`: direction, field count, size, state), rate limited and struck; names, chat, tags and paths are sanitized (`Sanitize`); the host validates every request (`CommandValidator`) and clients validate everything the host sends; the save sync is chunked and size capped; the join check covers scripts, project code and the mod message set.
- Mods: player scripts (`game/scripts`) are verified after compiling (`ModSandbox`): no IO, network, reflection, threads, process, environment, native code, clock or engine internals. Mods declare permissions in `scripts/mod.json` (`network`, `content`). New `IModNetwork` (typed, host-validated mod messages) and `IModContent` (read-only `scripts/content/`). The locked project backend keeps full access. C# modding stays on.
- `burger sec-test <project>`: sandbox escapes, path traversal and injection, fuzz, channel attacks, host authority, and a legit mod over the network.
- Multiplayer prototype (core only, no UI yet): `Burger.Core.Multiplayer`. Lockstep for one host and up to 8 humans: only commands travel (`move`, `declare_war`, `accept_peace`, `refuse_peace`, `white_peace`, `queue_production`, `event_option`, plus system commands `claim` and `release`). The host stamps each command with `hostTick + 2`, broadcasts it, and tells clients how many ticks it has run, so a client never runs past a command it has not got. The host owns pause and speed; clients vote and a majority of the humans decides. A checksum (country gold and manpower, division location and strength, RNG streams, who is human) is taken on the 1st of every month; on mismatch the host sends a full save and the client reloads. A leaving player's country goes back to the AI and shows an "AI" tag in the player list. Join code = host address and port in ten characters; the scripts folder is hashed at join and must match. `burger mp-test <project>` plays a host and three clients (one over TCP) through votes, commands, a forced desync and a leaver, and checks equal state at the end.
- Multiplayer UI and routing: host and join on the main menu, player panel, chat, toasts, country pick for joiners. Clock keys, unit moves, events, proposals and production now go through the session while one runs; new commands `respond` and queued `move`. See docs/multiplayer/multiplayer.md for what is still direct.
- Saves now carry what a human still has to answer (event queue and proposals) in an `x_multiplayer` section, together with the player list (a loaded save makes players pick their country again). Without it a reloaded game drifted from the uninterrupted one as soon as a human had an unanswered event. Not kept: named scopes of a queued event.
- Map cache: loading a map now keeps its slow products (province, terrain, height, tree and river arrays) in `<game>/cache/map/`. Each entry is keyed by a SHA-256 of the content of its source files and settings, so any change to a map file rebuilds just that entry. A game load of World500BCE drops from about 6.6 s to 2.5 s. Delete the folder or set `BURGER_NO_MAP_CACHE` to bypass it; it is git-ignored.
- Faster start (World500BCE, warm: about 9.4 s to about 2.6 s). More cache entries in `<game>/cache/`: map geometry, border scan, coast distance, terrain atlas, and the compiled C# backend and scripts (`cache/scripts/`, keyed by source text and engine build). The renderer's CPU work (atlas, rivers, terrain blend, map-data image) starts on worker threads as soon as the map is loaded, while the rest of the game still loads (`Game.MapLoaded`, `MapRenderer.Prewarm`). The log shows "Map renderer built in N ms".
- `--monitor N` (or `BURGER_MONITOR=N`) opens the game full screen on monitor N (0 = first); for testing on the second monitor.
- Main Menu (from the pause menu) no longer rebuilds the GPU map: the renderer is kept when the map size and locations match. "Reload files" and Retry still rebuild everything.
- Starting the game with a project folder (not its `game/` subfolder) now finds `game/` on its own. The load-error panel layout was fixed.
- Determinism: the shared `Game.Rng` is gone. `Game.Rngs` (`RngService`) gives every system its own named, seeded stream (`combat`, `events`, `characters`, `navy`, `politics`, `population`, `decisions`, `focus`, `tech`, `diplomacy`, `script`, `ai:TAG`). Inside a country AI tick all systems draw from that country's stream, so serial and parallel runs match. Stream positions are saved in `x_rng`. Seeded games give different worlds than before, but the same seed now always gives the same world. Mods: use `game.Rngs.<Name>` instead of `game.Rng`. New `burger validate-par <project>` checks serial == parallel (`projects/World500BCE_PTest/scorecard.txt`).
- Soldier and tank figures now stand on a soft circular shadow that lies on the slope under their feet. `FigureShadows` picks the shadow per model (`FigureShadows.Set(modelKey, shadow)`); implement `IFigureShadow` (it gets the pose, placement, up vector and camera) for a per-model or cast shadow. `FigureShadows.Enabled = false` turns them off; `BlobShadow` has `Size`, `Strength`, `Lift` and `Length`.
- Save and load are now exact: a game saved at any day and loaded into a fresh game plays on identical to the uninterrupted game. Fixed: saves rounded numbers to 4 decimals (now round-trip `"R"`), party popularity was re-normalized and rebuilt on load, the political modifier and wealth caches went stale (now rebuilt hourly and daily, under a lock), vacant parliament seats were not saved, and the game seed was not restored from the save (it seeds the parliament draws). `WarfareSystem` front hashes no longer use `HashCode.Combine` (random per process).
- `burger validate-par` now also plays a fourth world, `reload`: it saves mid-run, loads into a fresh game and plays on; scorecard lines `serial_eq_reload_*` check serial == reload. `World500BCE_PTest` is busier (imperator combat, laws, civil wars, independence, AI profiles, navy, country politics and diplomacy history): 104 of 104 lines within band over 8 seeds and 3650 days.
- Dynamic provinces: a base game system (`GeoDynamics`) that ticks monthly with the simulation on any project with a geography layer. Provinces split along fronts when a war occupies part of them, split in two balanced halves when their estimated population passes `split_population`, and merge back when the front disappears or the people dwindle. Changes are normal runtime operations (lineage, conservation, saved with the game) and never touch provinces that have authored events still to come. Tune or turn off in the geography `settings` block (`dynamic`, `split_population`, `merge_ceiling`, `min_age_days`, `front_min_age_days`, `max_changes_per_month`).
- Console: `geodebug` (highlights dynamic provinces), `geolist` (what changed), `geowar [n|off]` (test occupation), `geoskip [months]` (run the sim forward and report changes). Tool: `burger geo simulate <project> --years N [--war] [--split-population P]` runs headless and checks determinism, validity and save/load.
- Console: `geobase [on|off]` overlays the start-layout province borders (magenta) above the current province shapes.

## 2026-10-14
- Added opt-in historical geography: stable location atoms, dated provinces with split, merge and redraw, lineage, temporal properties, validation, runtime save section `x_geography`, `burger geo` tools and self-tests. See docs/game/GEOGRAPHY.md.
- Optional normal map: `map/normal.png` (tangent space, x east, y north, z up; name set by `normalmap` in `default.map`) adds fine hill shading. A map without one renders exactly as before. The earth1930 and isle bases now include one, made by `tools/earth/make_normal.py`.
- Map shader: bare rock on steep slopes, snow above a snow line that drops towards the poles, sky and sun light with a warm sun, and a soft distance haze on the flat map. Terrain mesh is now 3072 vertices wide by default (`mesh_width`), cut into chunks that are skipped when out of view.
- World500BCE: exaggerated heightmap, `height_scale = 7`, normal map, new seamless 2048 terrain textures.
- Terrain textures: the atlas cell follows the largest texture, capped at 1024 (never scaled lower), with a wrapped border so tile edges and mipmaps stay clean. Terrain types blend over a wider neighborhood with noise, so edges are no longer pixel stairs.
- Far-view speedup: zoomed out the terrain draws from a quarter-resolution mesh, the terrain blend collapses to one mip-mapped texture lookup, water closeness reads one texel, and MSAA switches off (it stays on up close). The continuous benchmark went from 56.7 to 146.5 fps average.
- Near-view speedup: the terrain blend kernel is baked once into two half-resolution weight textures (up to 8 terrain types; larger sets keep the old per-pixel loop), and the water normal uses an exact one-pass slope instead of three evaluations. Benchmark average 146.5 to 185.6 fps.
- Water is now part of the terrain mesh and shader instead of a flat tile (ported crossing-swell water shader): the open ocean is unpredictable (warped swells), lakes are completely still, coasts are calm and muted with a smooth blend from the open sea. Sea-level vertices lift with the waves; shallows show a sandy bed. Rivers read a half-resolution river texture. `--lookat lon,lat` (with `--zoom`) aims the camera for screenshots.

## Steadier country labels

- A name is only refitted when its own land, owner or text changes; any other map change leaves it exactly where it was.
- A refit that keeps the old curve inside the land and the letters nearly as big is skipped (a province changing hands elsewhere no longer nudges the name).
- A refit that does move a name glides to the new curve over 0.7 s instead of jumping.
- Letters follow unsnapped, smoothly blended directions, so they no longer step or flip angle at every corner while the camera moves.
- A name that is too big (letters over about 12% of the window height, gone at 24%) or mostly off screen (under 30% of its curve visible, full at 65%) fades out. It no longer stops growing at a fixed size.
